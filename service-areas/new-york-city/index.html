The city

What the work looks like in the city

City property runs tight. Driveways are short where they exist at all, buildings share entrances, and the useful surface is often the sidewalk and the steps rather than a wide open lot. That shapes every service RWB brings into the five boroughs.

In winter, that means snow removal in the city is frequently walkway and entrance work as much as plowing. The path from the street to the door is what people actually use, so it gets cleared and salted rather than left for later.

Parking is the other city-specific problem. One vehicle in the wrong place can stop a small lot from being plowed at all, which is why towing and snow work are handled by the same company here.

Once the weather turns, the city work shifts to junk removal out of apartments, basements and walk-ups, and to washing. There is more brick, concrete and stone stoop per property in the city than anywhere else in the area, which is power washing territory, while the more residential blocks of Queens and Staten Island bring siding and fencing into soft washing range.

Boroughs

All five boroughs

Brooklyn, Queens, Manhattan, The Bronx and Staten Island. The service list is identical in each. The kind of property is not.

Brooklyn

Row houses and short driveways, with a lot of the winter work happening on walkways and front steps. Small commercial lots behind businesses are the other regular call.

Queens

The widest mix in the city. Detached houses with real driveways, apartment property, and small business lots, which means snow, hauling and washing all come up across the borough.

Manhattan

Frontage, entrances and service areas rather than driveways. Access and timing decide how a job runs here more than the size of the property does.

The Bronx

Apartment property, houses on grades, and commercial frontage. Sloped surfaces get salted properly, because a clear surface on a hill is still a problem once it refreezes.

Staten Island

The most suburban borough. Longer driveways, more detached houses and more siding and fencing, so the work here has more in common with Nassau County than with Manhattan.

Residential and commercial

Houses, apartment property, buildings and business lots. Say which one you have when you call so the right setup arrives the first time.

Questions

New York City questions

Which boroughs do you cover?

All five. Brooklyn, Queens, Manhattan, The Bronx and Staten Island, plus Nassau County and Suffolk County on Long Island.

Do you clear sidewalks and building entrances?

Yes, and in the city that is often the bigger part of the job. Walkways, steps and entrances get cleared and salted alongside whatever driving surface there is.

Can you tow a car that is blocking a lot?

Yes, on property you are responsible for. Because RWB handles both towing and snow removal, the vehicle and the surface underneath it can be dealt with on the same visit.

Do you take junk out of apartments and walk-ups?

Yes. RWB does the lifting, which is usually the reason the item is still sitting in the apartment. Mention the stairs when you call.

Are you available overnight in the city?

RWB is open 24 hours and provides 24-hour emergency services across the whole service area, boroughs included.
