Suffolk

What the work looks like in Suffolk

Suffolk County is the eastern half of Long Island, and the properties get bigger as you go. Longer driveways, more ground around the house, detached garages, sheds, decks and a lot more fencing. Everything takes longer here simply because there is more of it.

That scale is the main thing that changes snow removal in Suffolk. A long driveway is a different job from a city apron, and open ground holds snow longer than a sheltered street does, so clearing and salting both matter more once the temperature drops overnight.

Bigger commercial lots come with the territory too, which usually means plowing overnight so the lot is clear and salted before anybody arrives.

The warm weather side is the same list, at the same scale. Long concrete and paver driveways for power washing, decks, fencing and siding for soft washing, and garages and sheds that produce a steady supply of junk removal work.

Property types

More ground to cover

Long driveways

Suffolk driveways can be several times the length of a city one, which changes how long a pass takes and how much salt a surface actually needs.

Commercial lots

Larger parking areas, access lanes and loading zones. Bigger lots take longer, so they get started earlier in a storm rather than left to the end.

Decks, fences and sheds

More exterior wood and siding per property than anywhere else in the service area, which is soft washing work, and outbuildings that fill with things that eventually have to go.

Suffolk is a large county. If your address is well out to the east and you are not sure it is in range, call 347-517-9920 and ask before assuming either way.

Suffolk County questions

Do you plow long driveways?

Yes. Long driveways are normal in Suffolk. They take longer than a city apron and usually need more salt, which is worth mentioning when you call.

How far east do you go?

Suffolk County is covered, but it is a big county. If your address is well out east, call 347-517-9920 and check rather than assuming either way.

Do you handle larger commercial lots?

Yes. Bigger parking areas, access lanes and loading zones are part of the commercial snow work, and larger lots get started earlier in a storm.

Do you wash decks and fencing?

Wood, fencing and siding are soft washing work, done at low pressure so the surface is cleaned rather than damaged. Concrete and paver driveways take pressure instead.
